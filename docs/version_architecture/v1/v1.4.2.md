# v1.4.2 — routing the second opinion

**Status:** in progress. Started 2026-09-20, after v1.4.1's smoke round closed 2/5
(`v1.4.1.md`, "v1.4.1 smoke test results" and "Round conclusion").

v1.4.1 proved the loop can produce a second opinion. It lost all three hard questions on
what SH did with one: it briefed alternatives by "don't overlap the last senior" instead of
"where the evidence of the act lives", and threw away an entity a second senior had proved
because it came from another feed. v1.4.2 is about that routing decision, plus the four
runner defects the round exposed.

## Changelog

- **Iterations per round: 12 -> 10** (`question_state.ROUND_ITERS`); rounds per senior stay
  at the 1000-tier's 8 (a brief raise to 10 was reverted the same day). A shorter round hands
  control back to SH more often, so a wrong path is caught sooner. Cut-off rounds are
  EXPECTED and are not a defect: at the cap the graph withdraws the search tools and asks
  for a final `submit_finding`, so the round ends on a forced summary of completed work, not
  mid-search. The iteration ceiling becomes 3 x 8 x 10 = 240.
- **Per-reply output budget: 32768 -> 65536** (`splunk_agent.token_limit_kwargs`). Probed
  against AI& on 2026-09-20: it accepts both 65536 and 131072 for `zai-org/glm-5.3`. GLM-5.3
  is a reasoning model and its hidden chain-of-thought is billed and capped as output, so
  two smoke3_r2 rounds returned `finish_reason=length` at exactly 32768 output tokens with
  no text and no tool call — the whole budget went on thinking and the round reached SH
  blank. Successful calls emit 47-2,667 tokens, so this is headroom, not a working budget;
  runaway generation is still bounded per round by `round_output_cap`.
- **Retire a dead direction immediately** (SH prompt, ALTERNATIVE SENIOR). RETIRE + SPAWN in
  the same turn as soon as the senior's OWN report shows its constraints cannot hold the
  answer (no such field, no content, no coverage). A round spent establishing that is a
  round well spent — what must not follow it is another round inside the same dead scope.
  The other two triggers (a candidate still UNVERIFIED after a verifying round; the same
  narrowed lead unsettled) stay.
- **Brief the replacement on the deviation.** The new direction must not re-walk the retired
  path: name fields, feeds or entities it did not touch, say where evidence of the ACT would
  sit if the first reading was wrong, and say what to avoid. A deviation may be small — the
  same feed through a different field counts; the same field again does not. The candidate
  is never handed over for confirmation, but any ENTITY the retired senior established is:
  "carrying a proven entity into the feed the question names is the most common way a stuck
  question is solved; dropping it because it was found elsewhere is how one is lost."
  (Q216: s2 proved the Coinhive host, SH discarded it as another feed's finding.)
- **R1 is about contribution, not about answering** (`SeniorDirective.r1_scope_alignment`
  description and the SH prompt's R1 walk). PASS = this round moved the identification of
  an entity the answer depends on, including from an angle SH did not ask for, or ruled one
  out with evidence. FAIL is reserved for work that cannot bear on the question. The
  word-by-word walk (entity / act / measure / reach) now grades the CHAIN AN ANSWER RESTS
  ON, via the premise audit, instead of sinking the round's grade. Leak-free general rule
  added in the same place: "an entity a senior found in another feed is a LEAD to test in
  the feed the question names — the question's wording binds where the MEASUREMENT is taken,
  not where the entity may be recognised."
- **A CLARIFY reply now really clears the cut-off block** (`QuestionState.clear_cap`, called
  from the loop's CLARIFY branch). The gate's message already offered CLARIFY as the cheap
  remedy but nothing cleared the flag, so Q216 spent ~5 turns clarifying into a gate that
  could not open. Message reworded to say the reply clears it.
- **Quotes may come from a CLARIFY reply** (`sh_loop` keeps `clarify_text` per senior and
  feeds it to `evidence_violations.reports_of`). SH was told to quote the senior word for
  word, then had its quotes rejected because the senior had said it in a clarify.
- **A question no longer ends with a CLARIFY reply unread** (`unread_clarify` joins `unread`
  in the loop's budget check). Q329 ended the instant s3 replied, so SH never read it.
- **An empty round says so** (`SeniorSession._fallback_report`). A senior that skips both
  `report` and `notes` used to reach SH with a blank "What it means" (Q329 s1 r2-r3). The
  fallback now uses `evidence`, then `answer`, and otherwise states plainly that nothing was
  filed.
- Tests: tier table, ceiling and token-cap expectations updated; suite 496 passed, 5 skipped.

## Root causes recorded for the v1.4.1 defects

- **Cut-off (`cap_hit`)**: `splunk_subagent` sets it when `step_count > max_iter`. At the
  cap the graph withdraws the search tools and asks for a final `submit_finding`, so the
  round ends on a forced summary rather than mid-search. The report is therefore honest but
  incomplete, which is why the ANSWER gate blocks on it.
- **Empty rounds**: `finish_reason=length` with `output_tokens=32768` in
  `log/temp/_smoke3_r2.out` — the senior's reasoning consumed the whole output budget and
  returned no text and no tool call. The agent node already retries once for
  `submit_finding`; what was missing was a readable report when that retry returns a finding
  with no `report` and no `notes`.
- **A VERIFIED quote may come from any senior's report** (`evidence_violations`, and
  `sh_loop`'s `reports_of(None)`). The check only searched the senior named as
  `source_senior`, so an answer built on s2 that rested on a premise s1 had established was
  rejected however faithfully it was quoted — v1.4.2's Q216 run lost three turns to exactly
  that. SH still names the real source in the audit line; the quote must still exist
  verbatim somewhere in the seniors' own text, so nothing can be invented.

  the 64K reply budget plus the honest fallback did their job.
- The cut-off gate no longer loops: one CLARIFY-free path to an ANSWER.
- What still loses the question: SH spawned s2 to CONFIRM s1's candidate
  ("confirm or refute that 45.77.53.176:3333 is the mining endpoint for 192.168.70.186")
  rather than to find the miner independently. The ALTERNATIVE SENIOR rule binds a
  hand-over; a PARALLEL spawn slipped past it, so both seniors ended up on the same lead and
  the Coinhive host in stream:dns — inside s2's own scope — was never looked for. Next
  change: apply the "reach its own answer, do not confirm mine" rule to every spawn while a
  candidate is already held, not just to a replacement.
- **LangSmith tracing off** (`agent/.env`: `LANGSMITH_TRACING=false`) until the monthly
  unique-trace quota resets in October — every run since 2026-09-19 was throwing 429s on
  every ingest and recording nothing.
- **A doubt now follows the senior, not its latest report** (`senior_report.carry_doubts`,
  tracked per senior in `sh_loop`, read by `evidence_violations` through `doubts_of`).
  Q216 (v1.4.2 r1) is the case: s1 round 1 wrote *"the 3333 record's byte profile (5.7MB in
  / 177B out) is download-like, so the record does not positively show the act -

  not reopen non-NVM validation"), and round 3 came back with every assumption VERIFIED and
  that line simply absent. The gate reads the latest report, so the doubt that should have
  blocked the answer had vanished. A doubt now leaves the list only when a later report
  names the same premise and marks it VERIFIED.
- **A VERIFIED line may not cite SH** (`_CIRCULAR` in `evidence_violations`). The same run
  closed the loop on itself: SH told s1 the attribution was settled externally, s1 wrote
  "established by SH outside this feed; not re-tested in NVM per instruction", and SH quoted
  that sentence as the evidence for its VERIFIED premise. An orchestrator's instruction is
  not evidence, however faithfully the senior wrote it down.
- Tests: `test_a_doubt_survives_a_report_that_simply_stops_mentioning_it`,
  `test_a_quote_that_cites_sh_is_not_evidence`; suite 498 passed, 5 skipped.

### What the Q216 v1.4.2 evidence actually showed (analysis, no code)

s2 did not hallucinate: every number in its report traces to a query it ran. What it did was
read its own evidence against its expectation. Its raw event for the 3333 session carries
`app=http`, `uri /images/logos.png`, `status 200` — an HTTP GET of a PNG on port 3333 — and
s2 reported that verbatim, then concluded "mining-consistent... disguised as HTTP
/images/logos.png". It also reversed the byte direction: Cisco NVM records the flow as
ibc=5,782,875 / obc=177 (5.7MB INBOUND, a download), while s2 read stream:tcp as "5.76MB
out / 39KB in" and used the upload reading as its mining signature. Neither senior
reconciled the contradiction, and SH never made them: it treated "s2 confirms s1" as
independent verification when s2 had been briefed to confirm s1's candidate in the first
place.

Open for the next version: a candidate that two seniors disagree about in DIRECTION or in
field values is not corroborated. SH has no rule that makes it reconcile conflicting
evidence between seniors, and CRITIC's `conflicts_other_senior` basis exists but is only
ever used against a report SH doubts, not against two reports that quietly disagree.

## Phase 2 — the premise ledger

> **Version note.** This work was drafted under the label `v1.5`. It is not a new
> version: v1.4.2 has not had its full scoreboard run, so per `CLAUDE.md` it is still

> spec is `v1.4.3`. Commits `a4f1b8c..b9078fd` carry a `v1.5` prefix from before the
> correction and were left as they are — the history is local, and rewriting fourteen
> commits buys nothing. The prose below keeps the `v1.5` label where it reads as the
> name of this phase's work.


**In progress.** Spec: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-20-premise-ledger-design.md`.
Plan: `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-09-20-premise-ledger.md`.

v1.5 replaces the senior's prose `## Assumptions` section, and the five regexes that
read it, with one runner-owned structured premise ledger. The root cause it targets is
v1.4.2's Q216 loss: a premise cannot be laundered out of existence by writing a
cleaner report next round, because the senior never re-emits the list.

### Changelog (premise ledger)

- **Probed GLM-5.3 on AI& for nested tool arguments (2026-09-20).** `submit_finding`
  took only flat primitives, and the whole ledger design depends on the senior being
  able to emit a list of objects. A one-off probe bound a tool with
  `new_premises: list[dict]` and `open_questions: list[str]` to `zai-org/glm-5.3` at
  `https://api.aiand.com/v1` and asked for two premises and one question.

  Result: **nested objects work.** The model returned
  `[{'kind': 'other', 'load_bearing': True, 'text': 'The flow is inbound-heavy.'},
  {'kind': 'definition', 'load_bearing': False, 'text': 'Port 3333 alone does not
  prove the activity.'}]` — every field present, both kinds correct, both booleans
  correct — and `open_questions` as a plain list of strings.

  So `submit_finding` types `new_premises` and `premise_updates` as `list`, and the
  JSON-string fallback in spec §5 is not needed. `finding._coerce_objects` still
  accepts a JSON string as cheap insurance against a provider changing its mind.

  (The probe first failed with `model_not_found` against `glm-5.3`: the id on AI& is
  `zai-org/glm-5.3`, as `run_all_v1.SENIOR_MODEL` already had it.)

- **`agent/v1/premise.py` — new (Tasks 1-5, `fe42a4f`..`ecdf6fb`).** The ledger:
  `PremiseDraft` / `PremiseUpdate` / `Premise` / `OpenQuestion`, `PremiseLedger`
  (filing, reading, transitions, rendering, `to_records`) and `dump_ledgers`. Ids are
  assigned by the runner and are global to the question, because a premise filed by
  one senior may be settled by another. `text` is immutable once filed. REFUTED is
  terminal. 30 tests.

- **Review fixes on the ledger (`67a0f1d`).** A code review of the above found four
  defects worth recording:

  1. **The quote check could not match a real quote.** `quote_supported` normalised
     with a markdown-only `_norm`, but a senior's tool output is JSON
     (`splunk_agent._format_result` returns `json.dumps({"results": ..., "meta": ...})`),
     so the corpus holds `{"dest_port": "3333"}` while a senior restates it as
     `dest_port=3333`. Nothing would have matched, every honest VERIFIED would have
     been rejected, and every answer would have deadlocked — v1.5 would have scored
     *worse* than v1.4.2 and looked like a model failure rather than a string-matching
     bug. Replaced by `_match_form`, which compares alphanumeric runs and ignores the
     punctuation between them; the 12-char floor is applied after normalisation so
     padding cannot buy length.
  2. **The carry-forward block ordered the impossible.** It listed every premise that
     was not VERIFIED, which includes REFUTED, and told the senior to settle each —
     but `apply()` refuses any update to a REFUTED premise. Now lists UNVERIFIED only;
     refuted premises are `render_refuted()`'s job.
  3. **Duplicates accumulated forever.** `add()` did not dedupe, and the carry-forward
     block actively invites re-filing. Settling one copy left its twin open for the
     rest of the question. `add()` now returns the existing premise for the same
     author and the same `_match_form(text)`.
  4. **VERIFIED was accepted with empty `evidence`** — the quote got a provenance
     check and a rejection note while the field that connects the quote to the premise
     got nothing. Now rejected with a note.

  Also deleted `blocking()` (dead, and its docstring falsely claimed it gates an
  ANSWER) and the unused `STATUSES` constant, and added tests for the forensic
  invariant that a *rejected* update must not append to `history`.

- **`premise._norm` deleted (`1b457ee`).** Left without a caller once `_match_form`
  took over, and `conversation.py`'s three uses are its own local copy inside
  `evidence_violations`, which a later task removes. It was alive only through its own
  test — the pattern the review flagged for `blocking()`.

- **`agent/v1/finding.py` — `submit_finding` files premises (Task 6, `dbf0e0f`).**
  Three new parameters (`new_premises`, `premise_updates`, `open_questions`) and their
  parsing in `parse_finding`, with `empty_finding` carrying the same keys so the
  prose-fallback shape stays identical to the structured one. `_coerce_objects`
  accepts a real list or a JSON string, so a provider that flattens nested arguments
  costs nothing. A malformed draft is dropped rather than fatal; an illegal status is
  dropped, leaving the premise at its old status, which is the safe direction.

  **Verified end to end against the live provider**, not just in unit tests: the
  generated schema for `new_premises` is
  `{"default": null, "items": {}, "title": "New Premises", "type": "array"}`, and
  `zai-org/glm-5.3` fills it with well-formed objects (correct `kind`, correct
  `load_bearing` booleans) which `parse_finding` turns into `PremiseDraft`s. This
  matters because Task 0 probed a *required* `list[dict]` while the real tool declares
  an *optional* `list` with a null default — a different schema, and the assumption
  was worth testing rather than inferring.

- **`agent/v1/conversation.py` + `agent/v1/sh_loop.py` — SH's half of the ledger
  (Tasks 8 and 9).** Done as one change: Task 8 deletes symbols `sh_loop.py` uses
  until Task 9, so splitting them leaves the suite red in between.

  `AuditLine`, `premise_audit`, `_audit_from_text`, the local `_norm`/`MIN_QUOTE_CHARS`,
  `unverified_audit` and `evidence_violations` are gone. `SeniorDirective` now carries
  `new_premises`, `premise_updates` and `answer_premise_ids` — SH writes into the same
  ledger as its seniors, as `author="sh"` — and `open_question_answers` is a list of
  `QuestionAnswer` (id + answer) instead of a bare list matched by index, which is how
  SH could answer question 2 twice and pass a count check.

  Four gates read the ledger now: `premise_audit_violations(entries, ledger)` (an
  ANSWER must cite existing ids, one of them `coverage`), `ledger_violations` (a
  load-bearing UNVERIFIED premise blocks while `unsure_remedy` has something to
  offer; a REFUTED one blocks with no escape at all), `sh_update_violations` (a
  VERIFIED/REFUTED verdict must quote a senior's report word for word via
  `quote_supported`, and may not cite SH), and `open_question_violations(entries,
  ledger)` matching by id. The `unverified_audit` block in `directive_violations` is
  deleted; the cut-off gate beside it is untouched.

  In `sh_loop.py`: one `PremiseLedger` per question, passed to every `SeniorSession`
  and returned as `run_question()["ledger"]`. The `doubts` dict and `carry_doubts` are
  gone — a premise that is not VERIFIED is the same thing, and the ledger already
  carries it forward. `render_wave` prints `render_table()` in place of the three
  regex `!!` warnings (unverified count, missing Coverage, missing Selection); absence
  of a `coverage` row is the warning. `_directive_text` no longer prepends a
  verify-first reminder on a WEAK/FAIL R4: `SeniorSession._message_for` prepends the
  senior's unresolved premises to *every* round, unconditionally, and the old prefix
  fired on SH's own grade — which is all-PASS whenever SH wants to answer.

  SH's ledger writes land only on a turn that passed every gate (after the
  `problems`/`continue` block). Consequence worth knowing: SH cannot file a premise
  and cite it in the same ANSWER turn — the citation is checked before the filing
  happens, so that turn is rejected and its writes discarded. SH has to file on an
  earlier (non-ANSWER) turn, or cite what a senior filed. That is the intended
  trade-off — nothing written from an invalid turn — but it costs a turn the first
  time SH discovers it.

  Prompts rewritten to match on both sides: SH's `AUDIT THE CHAIN` paragraph becomes
  `THE PREMISE LEDGER` plus the trace-and-file instruction; the gate bullets name the
  ledger, the UNVERIFIED block and the REFUTED no-escape block. `SENIOR_BRIEF` loses
  the `## Assumptions` and `## Open questions for SH` report sections (`## Ruled out`
  is now the template's last), and every prose reference to "your Assumptions" is
  re-pointed at `new_premises` / `premise_updates` / `open_questions`.

  Tests: `agent/v1/tests/` goes 563 -> 574 passing. Converted rather than dropped —
  each encodes a real failure from a past run — except four whose only subject was the
  legacy `"<premise> - VERIFIED: <where>"` string parsing or the `!!` regex warnings,
  which are superseded by the ledger tests.

- **SH's ledger writes moved BEFORE the gates, and filed ids auto-linked
  (`agent/v1/sh_loop.py`).** The Tasks 8+9 implementer flagged the consequence of the
  plan's ordering and was right to: with SH's writes after the gate block, an ANSWER
  that filed its own Coverage premise was rejected for citing an id that did not exist
  yet. Worse than it first looks - the runner assigns premise ids, so SH *cannot* know
  the id of a premise it is filing, and settling a premise and answering from it in one
  turn was impossible for the same reason. That is precisely the failure this version
  exists to delete: a gate refusing a turn for a reason the agent cannot act on, which
  is how v1.4.1 burned ten turns retiring the same senior.

  Two changes. The writes now run before the gates, so the gates judge the ledger the
  turn is actually asking for. And the runner appends the ids it has just assigned to
  an ANSWER's `answer_premise_ids`, because SH has no way to name them itself.

  Writing from a turn that is then rejected is safe, and deliberate: `add` dedupes on
  (author, text) so a re-issued turn refiles nothing, `apply` runs its own quote check
  and keeps the old status when it fails, and a premise only ever enters the ledger
  UNVERIFIED - which blocks rather than permits. The visible effect is that SH now gets
  the true rejection ("that premise is UNVERIFIED, settle it") instead of an
  unactionable one ("that id is not a premise"). Same block, opposite usefulness.

  Pinned by `test_sh_can_file_a_premise_and_cite_it_in_the_same_answer_turn` and
  `test_a_premise_sh_filed_survives_the_turn_that_was_rejected`.

- **Task 10: `senior_report.py` — the regex layer over the report's prose deleted.**
  Nothing called it any more once Tasks 8-9 moved reads onto the ledger. Gone:

  cut eating the sections SH grades on) no longer applies since those sections
  left the report entirely. `grep -rn` for every deleted symbol under `agent/`

  design) and the test file being edited in the same commit.

  Tests: 10 deleted (the doubt-carrying tests, the markdown `open_questions`
  tests, the Coverage/Selection premise tests, the `unverified_premises` tests) —
  577 -> 567 passing, 5 skipped. `truncate_words`, `novel_spl`, `stamp_header` and

- **Task 11: one `premise_ledger.json` per run (`agent/v1/run_all_v1.py`).** The runner
  collects each question's ledger (`run_question()["ledger"]`, plus the hint retry's

- **Final review fixes, before the first run (2026-09-20).** A full-diff review of
  `a4f1b8c..HEAD` returned CHANGES_REQUESTED with two CRITICALs. Both were invisible to
  the suite because every existing test either builds the ledger by hand or hand-feeds
  the worker's result dict. Found and fixed before any run was paid for:

  - **C1 — the senior half of the ledger was never wired in.**
    `SplunkWorkerPool._run` (`agent/v1/splunk_subagent.py`) builds its return dict by
    copying named keys out of `finding`, and `new_premises`, `premise_updates` and

    `SeniorSession.work` read them with `result.get(...)`, so on every real round all
    three were `None` -> `[]`. No senior premise could ever enter the ledger, the
    carry-forward block never printed, and `open_question_violations` could never fire —
    silently, with a green run and an SH-only `premise_ledger.json`. The three keys are
    now returned. Regression: `test_ledger_wiring.py` asserts `_run`'s source carries
    them and that both finding paths supply them.

  - **C2 — a circular quote was written before the gate that rejects it.** The runner

    rejected turn is not rolled back. `_CIRCULAR` lived only in `sh_update_violations`,
    so an update quoting SH as its own authority flipped the premise to VERIFIED, got
    the turn rejected, and left the laundered VERIFIED standing for the re-issued turn —
    the v1.4.2 Q216 loss reopened by the fix for the id-ordering defect. The regex moved
    to `premise.CIRCULAR` and `PremiseLedger.apply` now refuses it too;
    `conversation.py` imports the same object so the two cannot drift.

  - **Open-question hard gate was dead.** `sh_loop` called `ledger.answer(...)` before
    the gates, and `open_question_violations` computes what is owed from

    always empty and the gate never fired. The answer writes moved after the gates pass,
    and now only accept ids the addressed senior actually owns (SH answering s2's `q5`
    inside s1's entry used to close it).

  - **SH's refused premise updates are surfaced.** `apply()`'s notes were discarded for
    SH (seniors get them appended to the report). SH would re-send the same malformed
    update every turn and be blocked each time by an ANSWER gate naming a premise it
    believed settled. The notes are now appended to the turn's rejection.

  - **`open_questions` now goes through `_coerce_objects`** (`agent/v1/finding.py`), like
    the other two fields. A provider sending the list as a JSON string would otherwise be
    iterated character by character, filing one open question per character. Masked by
    C1; live the moment C1 was fixed.

### What the ledger changed vs phase 1

**One ledger instead of two lists and five regexes.**

v1.4.2 kept premises in two places that never met: the senior's `## Assumptions`
markdown, scraped by `open_doubts` / `carry_doubts` / `unverified_premises` /
`has_coverage_premise` / `has_selection_premise`, and SH's own `premise_audit` list of
`AuditLine`. v1.5 has one `PremiseLedger` per question that both sides write to, SH as
`author="sh"`.

| | phase 1 | phase 2 (ledger) |
|---|---|---|
| Where premises live | prose in the report | schema fields on `submit_finding` |
| Who owns the list | the senior re-wrote it each round | the runner; the senior emits only new premises and id-keyed updates |
| Dropping one | silent, and indistinguishable from settling it | impossible — the senior never re-emits the list |
| Matching across rounds | `_label()` on the first four words | a stable runner-assigned id |
| VERIFIED | the word "VERIFIED" in a bullet | a quote the runner finds in tool output that author actually received |
| A disproven premise | no such state | `REFUTED`, terminal, and no budget state lets an answer through it |
| Carried to the next round | only via `carry_doubts`, and never shown to the senior | the runner prepends every unresolved premise, verbatim, to every round |
| SH's open questions | a bare list matched by index | `QuestionAnswer{id, answer}`, matched by id |

**The failure this targets.** v1.4.2's Q216: s1 flagged the 3333 flow's byte profile as
download-like and UNVERIFIED in rounds 1 and 2; SH commanded a restate-only round ("Do
not reopen non-NVM validation"); round 3 came back all-VERIFIED with the line simply
gone, and SH quoted s1 quoting SH as its evidence. Three separate defences failed at
once — the doubt gate read only the latest report, `_label` could not match a reworded
premise, and nothing checked that a quote traced to real data. All three are now one
mechanism.

**What got harder on purpose.** A REFUTED premise blocks an ANSWER with no escape, where
v1.4.2 let SH answer on an unverified premise once its budget ran out. A question can now
end `NO_ANSWER` where it would previously have submitted something. That is the intended
trade: a known-false answer scores zero anyway and poisons the case file for every later
question in the run.

**What got easier.** The 600-word report cap no longer competes with the premise list,
because premises left the report. And SH is told the true reason an answer is blocked
("that premise is UNVERIFIED, settle it") rather than an unactionable one.

**Measured** — see the next section.

The ledger cost 2.4x more and scored the same zero, by a worse route. What it bought and
what it broke are separable, and both are in `premise_ledger.json`.

**What worked — the senior side does exactly what the spec wanted.** The ten senior-filed
premises were settled from real query output, and `p2` was **REFUTED**: s1 filed the
`iexeoler.exe` misspelling as its prime miner lead in round 1, then killed it in round 2
with `{"pn": "iexeplorer.exe", "dp": "8080", "da": "192.168.9.30", ...}` — internal C2
traffic, not mining. Phase 1's failure mode (a premise laundered out of existence by a
cleaner report next round) did not recur: no premise changed text, and none vanished.

**What broke — SH re-filed the same three premises six times.** 18 of the 28 premises are
SH's, and they are six copies of one coverage/selection/definition triplet, filed at
rounds 4, 5, 8, 10, 16 and 18. Three distinct claims, eighteen ids. The cause is a
deadlock the auto-link fix (`9f51825`) introduced without anyone noticing:

1. the runner assigns ids, so SH cannot name a premise it is filing *this* turn in
   `premise_updates` — the id does not exist yet;
2. the auto-link puts every freshly filed premise into `answer_premise_ids`, so it is
   immediately a load-bearing UNVERIFIED blocker on the very ANSWER that filed it;
3. `ledger_violations` blocks, telling SH to "COMMAND s1 to settle them" — which s1
   cannot do, because `render_for_senior` filters on `p.author == author` and s1 is never
   shown an SH-authored premise, or its id;
4. SH's prompt says to file "every premise it rests on that **no one has**", so on the
   next attempt it rewords slightly and files three more. `add()`'s dedupe keys on
   `_match_form(text)`, which a rewording defeats
   ("The measured duration should come from the only flow in the feed matching..." ->
   "The duration to report should come from the single dp=3333 stratum flow...").

**SH verified all 15 of its own settled premises itself.** No senior ever settled another
senior's premise — the cross-author path the design kept is unreachable for the same
`render_for_senior` reason. And 6 of SH's 19 settling quotes are a senior's *prose
restatement* rather than a result: `p4`'s evidence is the sentence "Coverage: the only
Monero-generation indicators in this feed are the single stratum-3333 flow and the
Coinhive browser flows". The prompt says "a senior citing a rule is not a result"; nothing
enforces it, because `quote_supported` only asks whether the string appears in the corpus.

**The deepest finding: a quote can be real, non-circular, and still not answer the
premise.** `p3` is the case, and it is why 112 is wrong. s1 filed:

> The 'seconds generating Monero' is a duration computed in SPL from the miner's flow
> records using fss/fes; whether it is the wall-clock span max(fes)-min(fss) or the sum of
> per-flow durations sum(fes-fss) **is unresolved and changes the answer**.

SH marked it VERIFIED in round 4, quoting
`` `dp=3333 | stats min(fss) max(fes) ... | eval duration_seconds=...` -> duration_seconds=112 ``.
That quote is genuine output of a search someone ran, and it does not cite SH, so both
gates pass — but it settles nothing. It reports a number computed under *one* of the two
readings s1 said were unresolved. The senior named the exact ambiguity that loses the
question, and the ledger closed it without resolving it.

No string check can catch this: the quote is responsive-looking, real and on-topic. This
is the case `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-20-validation-agent-design.md` (v1.4.3) exists
to handle, and it is now backed by a concrete example rather than a hypothesis.

Two of spec 2's provisional parameters are refuted by this data and must not be used as
written:

- **`load_bearing` cannot be the validator trigger.** At 89% it selects almost everything.
  The field is not discriminating because both SH and the seniors mark nearly every
  premise load-bearing, which is the honest reading of "the answer breaks without it".
- **"UNVERIFIED for ≥2 rounds" is not a rare event.** The median premise sits UNVERIFIED
  for exactly 2 rounds, so the trigger fires on roughly half of all premises. With 28
  premises that is far past the "max 3 validators per senior" budget.

A trigger that fires on the `p3` shape specifically — a premise whose own text names an
unresolved choice ("unresolved", "changes the answer", "either X or Y") — is worth more
than either, and costs nothing to detect. Recorded here for spec 2 rather than built now.

### Changelog — fixes from the Q216 ledger run (2026-09-21)

Four changes, all aimed at the re-file loop above. The senior side needed nothing: it
already worked.

- **One OPEN premise per kind per author** (`PremiseLedger.add`). Text dedupe keys on
  `_match_form`, which a rewording defeats — and SH reworded every time. The kinds are
  singular by construction (the prompt asks for *a* coverage premise, *a* selection
  premise, *a* definition premise), so a second open premise of a kind the author already
  has open is a re-file: `add` hands back the one it owns instead of minting an id. Once
  the first is settled the kind is free again, so a question can still raise a genuinely
  new selection premise later.

- **A premise can be filed and settled in one turn.** `PremiseDraft` gains optional
  `quote` and `evidence`; when `quote` is present `add` runs the same `apply` path, so the
  same `quote_supported`, `CIRCULAR` and non-empty-`evidence` checks decide the outcome.
  This is the deadlock's only real exit: the runner owns the ids, so an author *cannot*
  name a premise it is filing this turn in `premise_updates`, and the auto-link makes that
  premise an instant load-bearing blocker on the very ANSWER that filed it. Nothing is
  weakened — a draft quote that is in no result, or that cites SH, leaves the premise
  UNVERIFIED exactly as an update would.

- **A senior is shown the open load-bearing premises other authors filed**
  (`render_for_senior`). The block used to filter on `p.author == author`, so
  `ledger_violations`' "COMMAND s1 to settle them" named an action s1 could not take: it
  had never seen the premise and did not know the id existed. The run settled zero
  premises senior-to-senior for this reason while SH verified all fifteen of its own. Only
  load-bearing and still-open premises are carried; anything else is noise in a round.

- **SH's prompt now says to read the ledger before filing.** It said "file every premise
  it rests on that no one has", which SH obeyed literally on every ANSWER attempt. It now
  says to cite the existing id and settle it, that a second open premise of a kind is read
  as a re-file, and that a premise it can already settle should carry its quote.

`add` also takes an optional `notes` list so a refused draft-quote reaches the author,
the way `apply`'s notes already do.

Tests: `test_ledger_refile_loop.py` (11 new) pins all three code changes, using the
verbatim reworded pair from the run. Three tests in `test_premise.py` asserted the old
behaviour and were updated — two filed two same-kind premises from one author only to get
two ids, and one asserted the author-only carry-forward filter. 582 passed, 5 skipped.

**Not yet measured.** No run has been made since these changes. The next Q216 run is their
acceptance test: the signal to read is SH's premise count, which was 18 for three claims.

### Q216 ledger re-run after the fixes (`log/temp/v1.4.2_ledger_Q216_r2/`, 2026-09-21)

**The loop is gone.** Every mechanical measure moved the right way, and the ledger is now
cost-neutral against the pre-ledger baseline.

| | phase 1 (no ledger) | ledger r1 | **ledger r2 (fixed)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Answer | 112 — wrong | none | 112 — wrong |
| Cost | $0.86 (SH $0.31 / sr $0.55) | $2.03 (SH $0.45 / sr $1.58) | **$0.88 (SH $0.14 / sr $0.74)** |
| SH turns / waves | — | 22 / 11 | **8 / 4** |
| Iterations | — | 79 / 240 | **36 / 240** |
| Premises | n/a | 28 (SH 18) | **3 (SH 0)** |
| ANSWER blocks | — | 6 | **0** |
| Turn rejections | 6 | 6 | **0** |

SH's own cost fell 3.2x, because it stopped re-filing. Three premises, one per kind, all
filed by s1 in round 2, all settled from real query output by round 4.

**But the answer is still 112, and the ledger certified the error.** This is the finding
that matters, and it is sharper than r1's.

s1's coverage premise `p1` reads, in its own words:

> In source=cisconvmflowdata, Monero generation can surface four ways: (a) a process-name
> census anomaly — searched...; (b) a destination port in the Monero pool range —
> searched, full 22-port list read, only 3333 present with exactly 1 event; **(c) flows to
> pool IP 45.77.53.176 on any port — NOT yet searched**; (d) a sustained byte-volume
> signature — only partially examined.

It was marked **VERIFIED**, and the quote it was verified with is the result of searching
route (c):

```json
{"da": "45.77.53.176", "count": "4832", "first_start": "1534758533",
 "last_end": "1534766374", "sa": ["192.168.105.214", "192.168.24.128", "192.168.70.186"],
 "dp": ["3333", "443", "80"], ...}
```

The premise says route (c) was not searched. The quote is route (c), and it returns **4,832
flows across three hosts and three ports**, not the single `dp=3333` event the answer rests
on. The quote does not merely fail to support the premise — it refutes it, and names the
coverage gap that makes 112 wrong. `p2` then selects that one flow out of the 4,832,
quoting a result that shows only that the flow exists.

Both gates passed, correctly by their own terms: the quote is genuine output of a search
s1 really ran (`quote_supported`), and it does not cite SH (`CIRCULAR`). **Nothing in the
runner reads the premise and the quote together**, because nothing can — that is a
comprehension task, not a string test.

**The other half: there is no independent verifier.** In r2, s1 filed all three premises
and verified all three itself; SH filed none and settled none. In r1 the mirror image: SH
filed 18 and verified 15 of its own. Both runs are self-certification, by different
parties. The ANSWER gate is satisfied by citing a premise whose author is also its only
witness. Keeping the prompt swinging between "SH files more" and "SH files less" cannot
fix this — neither party is disinterested.

That is exactly what `2026-09-20-validation-agent-design.md` (v1.4.3) specifies: an agent
shown one premise and its evidence, nothing else, asked only whether the evidence supports
the claim. `p1` is the ideal test case — a validator given that text and that quote needs
no case knowledge to answer "no".

### Calibration for v1.4.3, revised on r2

r1's numbers were measured on a ledger inflated by the re-file loop and should not be used.
r2 is the realistic population:

| quantity | r1 (inflated) | **r2 (use this)** |
|---|---|---|
| Premises per question | 28 | **3** |
| `load_bearing` share | 25/28 = 89% | **3/3 = 100%** |
| Rounds UNVERIFIED before settling | median 2, range 1–19 | **1–2** |
| Self-verified premises | SH 15/15 | **s1 3/3** |
| Cross-author settles | 4 (all SH) | **0** |

With three premises per question, spec 2's "max 3 validators per senior" is no longer at
risk of saturating, and validating **every** load-bearing premise once is affordable — at
r2's rates a validator round is a small fraction of $0.74. So the §7 trigger can be much
simpler than the spec's "UNVERIFIED ≥2 rounds with the candidate unchanged":

> **Validate every load-bearing premise at the moment it is settled**, before the status is
> allowed to stick. That catches `p1` at exactly the point the error entered, rather than
> waiting for a staleness signal that a self-certifying author never emits.

`load_bearing` remains non-discriminating (100% here), which is fine under this trigger —
it selects everything, and everything is three.
