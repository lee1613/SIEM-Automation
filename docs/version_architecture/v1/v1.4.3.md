# v1.4.3 — the validation agent

**In progress.** Spec: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-20-validation-agent-design.md`
(spec 2 of 2). Opened 2026-09-21, straight after the Q216 ledger runs recorded in
`v1.4.2.md`, because those runs showed the premise ledger cannot catch its own worst
failure.

v1.4.2 phase 2 made premises structural: the runner owns them, they cannot be dropped by
omission, and a VERIFIED status needs a quote from a result the author really received.
Two Q216 runs then showed what that still does not buy. The author of a premise is also
its only witness, and a quote can be genuine, non-circular, and still not establish the
claim it is attached to. v1.4.3 adds the one party the design has never had: a reader who
wants nothing.

## Changelog

- **The validation agent (`agent/v1/validator.py`, new).** An agent handed one settled
  premise and the evidence offered for it, and nothing else — not the question, not the
  reports, not the candidate, not the other premises. It re-checks the claim against the
  data itself and files one `premise_updates` verdict, under the same quote and
  circularity rules as any other author, with its quote checked against its OWN tool
  output.

  It exists because both Q216 ledger runs failed the same way from opposite sides: in r2
  s1 filed all three premises and verified all three itself; in r1 SH filed 18 and
  verified 15 of its own. The ANSWER gate is satisfied by a premise whose author is also
  its only witness, and no prompt fixes that — neither party is disinterested.

  What it has to catch is r2's `p1`: a coverage premise whose own text says route (c) was
  *"NOT yet searched"*, marked VERIFIED quoting the result of searching route (c) and
  returning 4,832 flows where the answer assumed one. `quote_supported` passes (the quote
  is real) and `CIRCULAR` passes (it does not cite SH). Reading a premise against its
  quote is comprehension, not a string test.

- **Settle-time trigger, replacing the spec's staleness trigger**
  (`sh_loop._run_validators`). Spec 2 §3.1 originally fired on a load-bearing premise that
  had been UNVERIFIED for two rounds with the candidate unchanged. r2 settled every
  premise within two rounds of filing it, so that trigger would never have fired. The
  runner now validates a load-bearing premise the moment it leaves UNVERIFIED, before the
  status is allowed to stick. SH is not asked and cannot decline.

  The senior is **not** retired, unlike the spec's version — that suited a trigger firing
  late in a senior's life; this one fires the round after a premise is filed, so retiring
  would end every senior in round 2. Validators run as their own parallel wave inside the
  same SH turn, and their verdicts reach SH with that wave's reports.

- **Budget** (`MAX_VALIDATORS_PER_QUESTION = 6`, `VALIDATOR_ITERS = 8`). Six because r2
  produced three load-bearing premises; it exists to bound a pathological question, not to
  ration a normal one. A premise is validated once, not once per wave.

- **Failure paths all leave the premise alone.** A validator that files no verdict, files
  several, quotes something it did not run, cites SH, or crashes in transport leaves the
  status untouched and says so in SH's next turn. `_timed` turns a crash into an
  `api_failed` dict with none of `validate()`'s keys, so the premise is read from the job
  map rather than the result.

- **`SPECIALISTS["validator"]`** carries the prompt; `parse_specialist_tag` deliberately
  does not match `[validator]`, because only the runner spawns one.

Tests: `test_validator.py` (12) builds on r2's `p1` verbatim — briefing contents, the id
and kind being withheld, the placeholder-id substitution, and every refusal path.
`test_sh_loop.py` gains 4 integration tests: a validator fires without SH being asked and
overturns the author's own VERIFIED, the briefing carries neither the question nor the
candidate, a refuted premise blocks the answer resting on it, and a premise is validated
once. Two `test_sh_loop` doubles were taught the validator role so validator calls stop
counting as senior rounds. 598 passed, 5 skipped, ruff clean.

**Not yet measured.** No run has been made on v1.4.3. The next Q216 run is its acceptance
test, and the signal to read is whether a validator refutes `p1` — the premise that made
112 look settled.

## Q216 acceptance run (`log/temp/v1.4.3_validator_Q216_r1/`, 2026-09-21)

**The validator works. It was then routed around.**

| Answer | 112 — wrong | 112 — wrong |
| Cost | $0.88 (SH $0.14 / sr $0.74) | **$1.88 (SH $0.20 / sr $1.68)** |

| Premises | 3 | 7 |

### What worked

- **`v1` overturned its author: `p1` VERIFIED → REFUTED.** SH filed the coverage premise
  and verified it; an agent shown nothing but that claim and its evidence refuted it,
  quoting its own full-feed scan of `dh` across all 78,459 events — CoinHive traffic on a
  non-port route the coverage claim had not covered. This is exactly the class of error no
  string check can reach, caught by the one party that wanted nothing.
- **`v2` confirmed `p2`**, so the verdict is not a rubber stamp in either direction.
- **The CoinHive lead surfaced**: `p5` records "6 HTTPS flows to coinhive.com/ws0XX.
  coinhive.com **totaling 1758 seconds**". The correct answer is 1666. The run found the
  right family of evidence and discarded it.
- Two earlier fixes confirmed live: seniors now file open questions at all (`q1`–`q5`
  exist — before the C1 fix `open_questions` never left the worker), and the open-question
  hard gate fires ("s1 is waiting on q1, q2 — answer each by id"). `CIRCULAR` fired twice
  on SH's own updates, and those refusals reached SH because `apply`'s notes are now
  surfaced.

### How the answer got through anyway

Three separate moves, each individually legal:

1. **SH re-framed the refuted premise.** `p1` (coverage) was REFUTED, so SH filed `p6`, a
   fresh coverage premise. `ledger_violations` blocks an ANSWER that **cites** a REFUTED
   premise; SH cited `p6` instead. The one-open-premise-per-kind guard does not apply,
   because a REFUTED premise is not open — by design, so a question is not left with a
   kind permanently spent.
2. **Nothing validated the replacements.** Validators run after a wave. `p6` and `p7` were
   both settled by SH on turn 10 — the ANSWER turn — and no wave follows an ANSWER, so no
   validator ever saw them. `p7` was filed *and* verified in that one turn, through the
   file-and-settle path added for the re-file loop.
3. **The instruction was laundered through an open question.** `p5`'s own text reads:
   *"per SH's q3 guidance the act is the unique stratum-style service connection"*. SH
   answered s2's open question in a way that discarded the CoinHive direction; s2 then
   filed that conclusion as its own premise and verified it from real data. `CIRCULAR`
   reads the *quote*, not the premise text, so a premise that rests on SH's instruction
   passes as long as its quote is genuine.

Every guard held on its own. The combination did not.

## Changelog — closing the two bypasses (2026-09-21)

Both holes the acceptance run found, fixed. The third move (SH steering a senior through
an open-question answer, so the senior files SH's conclusion as its own premise) is
**recorded and not fixed** — see below.

- **A validator's refutation blocks the ANSWER whether it is cited or not**
  (`conversation.ledger_violations`). The gate read `answer_premise_ids`, so SH escaped
  `p1`'s refutation by filing `p6` and citing that. A premise filed load-bearing and then
  shown false does not stop mattering because the next turn stops mentioning it.

  The escape is real but must be independent: a premise of the same `kind`, VERIFIED **by
  a validator**. One the author verifies itself does not clear the block, which is exactly
  what SH did on the answering turn.

  Self-refutation is deliberately exempt (`premise.is_validator`). An author killing its
  own premise is healthy investigation — Q216 r1's s1 filed the `iexeplorer.exe` lead and
  refuted it itself the next round — and blocking on that would punish the one behaviour
  the ledger is trying to encourage. Only a validator's refutation means the party that
  wanted the answer was overruled by one that wanted nothing.

- **An ANSWER's cited premises are validated before the gates read the ledger**
  (`sh_loop._run_validators`, `pre_status=None` / `cited=`). Validators ran after a wave,
  and no wave follows an ANSWER, so `p6` and `p7` — both settled by SH on turn 10, `p7`
  filed and verified in that one turn — were never seen. Verdicts now land before
  `ledger_violations` runs, so the gates read them on the same turn.

Tests: `test_validator_bypass.py` (4) pins the gate, including that an author refuting its
own premise blocks nothing; `test_sh_loop.py` gains an integration test that a premise SH
files and settles on the ANSWER turn is still briefed to a validator. 603 passed, 5
skipped, ruff clean.

### Not fixed: laundering through an open question

`p5`'s text reads *"per SH's q3 guidance the act is the unique stratum-style service
connection"*. SH answered s2's open question in a way that discarded the CoinHive
direction; s2 filed that conclusion as its own premise and verified it from real data.
`CIRCULAR` reads the **quote**, and the quote was genuine — the instruction is in the
premise's *text*.

Left alone deliberately. A senior acting on its orchestrator's scope guidance is the
design working, not a defect; the boundary rule exists precisely so SH can direct scope.
What makes this case bad is that the guidance was wrong, and the ledger recorded the
senior's agreement as independent support for it. Distinguishing the two needs a reader,
not a regex — which is the validator, pointed at premises whose text cites an
instruction. Worth doing once there is a second example; one is a hypothesis.

## Q216 r2, after the bypass fixes (`log/temp/v1.4.3_validator_Q216_r2/`, 2026-09-21)

**Answer 112 again — but this time the system worked as designed and was still wrong.**
$1.29 (SH $0.19 / senior $1.10), 9 turns, 3 waves, 6 premises, 6 validators.

The gate did exactly what it was built to do:

- `p1` (s1's coverage premise) was **REFUTED by `v4`**, an independent validator.
- The new gate blocked the ANSWER on it whether cited or not.
- SH filed `p6`, a replacement coverage premise — and **`v6` independently verified it**,
  which is the escape the gate defines. The ANSWER was then legitimately allowed.

So the bypass is closed: SH can no longer self-certify its way past a refutation. What
this run shows instead is the next layer down — **an independent validator agreed with a
coverage claim that is still wrong.** The validator is not omniscient; it is one more
reader, and a blind one. Being overruled by it is now hard to fake, but being *confirmed*
by it is not the same as being right.

### A defect this run found in the fix itself

The log contains:

```
the runner refused a premise update: --- v6 | INDEPENDENT VALIDATION of p6 | VERIFIED -> VERIFIED
```

The ANSWER-turn verdicts were appended to `ledger_notes`, which the gate block converts
into turn rejections — so **every successful validation rejected the turn it belonged
to**, and was mislabelled as a refusal of SH's update. Three of this run's ANSWER blocks
trace to this rather than to any real gate. Fixed: verdicts are kept in their own list,
attached to the rejection when there is one (SH cannot act on a validator-caused block
without reading what the validator found) and appended to the conversation otherwise.
Regression: `test_a_validators_verdict_is_not_reported_as_a_refused_update`.

### Validator verdict rate, third reading

| run | spawned | usable verdicts |
|---|---|---|
| v1.4.3 r1 | 4 | 2 |
| **v1.4.3 r2** | **6** | **4** |

Both r2 failures were again *"quoted something it did not run"* — the validator quoting the

# Revision — SH stamps, the runner triggers, the AA replaces (2026-09-21)

**Status: designed, not built.** This revises v1.4.3's core mechanism rather than
patching it. Agreed in interview after reading the r1/r2 ledgers, the v1.4.3 runs and the
grade record. Nothing here is implemented yet.

## Why the shipped version has to change

Two readings of the run data, neither available when spec 2 was written.

**1. R4 is a permission slip, not a measurement.** Every grade SH gave, across three runs
and six seniors:

```
v1.4.2_ledger_Q216_r2          v1.4.3_validator_Q216_r2      v1.4.2_ledger_Q216_r1
 rd1 PASS PASS FAIL FAIL        rd1 PASS PASS WEAK FAIL       s1 rd1-3  R4 = WEAK WEAK WEAK
 rd2 PASS PASS WEAK FAIL        rd2 PASS PASS WEAK WEAK       s1 rd4-5  R4 = PASS PASS
 rd3 PASS PASS WEAK FAIL        rd3 PASS PASS PASS PASS  ANS  s2 rd1-2  R4 = WEAK WEAK
 rd4 PASS PASS PASS PASS  ANS                                 s2 rd3-4  R4 = PASS PASS
                                                              s3 rd1 FAIL -> rd2 PASS
```

R4 flips to PASS on the turn SH stops investigating. No exceptions. `premise.py:332`
predicted this in prose ("SH grades all-PASS when it wants to answer"); this is the
measurement.

**2. The trigger spec 2 would have used fires when it is not needed.** The interview's
first candidate was `R1/R2/R3 = PASS and R4 != PASS` — retire, then validate. Against the
data that conjunction occurs **zero times in both r2 runs** and twice in r1. R3 and R4
move together: a senior firms up its candidate and settles its own premises in the same
round (r2 rd3 to rd4, where `p2` goes VERIFIED and R3 goes WEAK to PASS on one turn). The
window where a senior holds a submittable candidate *and* unsound ground never opens.
Same failure shape as the staleness clock §3.1 replaced: a condition describing a state
the system passes through too fast to catch.

## The design

### Roles, hard-separated

| party | may | may not |
|---|---|---|
| senior | file premises, verify them from its own results, raise open questions | — |
| SH | file premises, **stamp** a senior's verifications, nominate, grade, route | **verify any premise** |
| validator | verify or refute one premise from its own searches | see the question, reports, candidate, or other premises |

SH losing the power to verify is the largest single change. In r1 SH settled **19 of 28**
premises — all 18 it authored, plus s1's `p3`, s2's `p20` and s3's `p27`. The premises
that lost Q216 (`p10`/`p11`/`p12`, the triplet that discarded the CoinHive reading) were
filed by SH and verified by SH in the same round. SH has no Splunk access and never will,
so "SH verified it" has always meant "SH read a report and decided" — which is the stamp,
and the stamp is explicitly not an edit.

### The stamp

When a senior's report **newly** claims a premise VERIFIED, SH must record, per premise:
*does this quote establish this claim as written — true or false*, with a reason.

- **Annotation, never an edit.** A false-stamped premise stays VERIFIED in the ledger.
  Status belongs to the quote rule and the validator; the stamp is SH's reading of it.
- **Once, on first claim.** Premises settled in an earlier turn are not re-stamped.
- **Mandatory.** A turn leaving a new verification unstamped is rejected.

It is due the round the verification is claimed, not at ANSWER. For r2 that puts `p1`'s
stamp at round 3, where SH graded `R3=WEAK` and was still investigating — one round before
the moment R4 has flipped to PASS in every run we have.

### R4 becomes a runner-enforced ceiling

SH cannot write `PASS` while either holds:

- any load-bearing premise authored by that senior is UNVERIFIED, or
- any stamp on that senior's newly-verified premises is false.

A REFUTED premise forces `FAIL`. SH may always grade lower than the ceiling, never above.
R4 now **records**; it triggers nothing. The clause "the ANSWER gate is the ledger, not
this grade" comes out of its description — it taught SH the grade was inert, and SH agreed.

### Trigger: a false stamp, alone

No clock, no conjunction with R1/R2/R3. A false stamp is SH stating in writing that a
specific premise's evidence does not establish its claim; that is a defect the moment it
is written. Requiring a candidate to be ready first only waits for the senior to finish
tidying up — which is when it settles its own premises.

```
false stamp -> retire that senior -> SH nominates ONE premise -> one validator
```

The nomination is drawn from premises SH stamped false, **or** premises still UNVERIFIED.
It may not range wider: the false stamp is what fired the mechanism, so letting SH aim the
validator elsewhere would leave the triggering doubt unexamined.

Requiring `R1/R2 = PASS` was also backwards — it exempted a senior that was both off-scope
and unsound while retiring one that was merely unsound.

### The validator gains a second mode

- **With an offered quote:** does this evidence establish this claim as written?
- **Without one** (nominating an UNVERIFIED premise): settle this claim from the data.

`brief_for` already omits the evidence block when there is no quote; only the prompt needs
to branch. The second mode is worth having: handed r2's `p1` — *"(c) flows to pool IP
45.77.53.176 on any port — NOT yet searched"* — with nothing attached and told to settle
it, the obvious move is to go search route (c). That is what `v1` did in v1.4.3 r1 with
its full-feed `dh` scan, which is the one time this architecture found the right lead.

### Verdicts

| verdict | response |
|---|---|
| VERIFIED | SH's doubt independently dismissed. Premise stands, SH may proceed. |
| REFUTED | Hard block. SH spawns an **alternative agent** on fresh ground. |
| UNVERIFIED | Treated as REFUTED — two readers could not stand the claim up. |

### The alternative agent (spec 2 §4, finally built)

Spawned after a REFUTED verdict. Carries `deviation` and `inherited_entities` (SH fills)
plus the runner-injected refuted-premise block. The SPAWN is rejected if `deviation` is
empty. §4.3 stands unchanged: divergence is observed, not checked.

`render_refuted()` is wired into **every** senior's carried brief, not only the AA's spawn.
This also closes a live defect: `render_for_senior` excludes REFUTED premises and justifies
it with *"render_refuted() already tells the senior which premises are dead"* — a function
that has never had a caller. Today no senior is told which premises are dead.

### Budget is the senior pool

Retirement consumes a slot, so the mechanism self-caps:

```
s1 -> false stamp -> retire + 1 validator -> AA = s2
s2 -> false stamp -> retire + 1 validator -> AA = s3
s3 -> false stamp -> retire + 1 validator -> no slots left
```

`TIERS[1000]["seniors"] = 3`, so at most 3 validators per question, with no constant.
**`MAX_VALIDATORS_PER_QUESTION` is deleted.** r2 used 1 senior of 3, so in practice this
is 1 validator — the number the budget concern asked for.

**Accepted incentive cost:** stamping false costs SH a senior. This is the same pressure,
in the same direction, at the same moment, that turned R4 into a permission slip. It is
accepted because every stamp is on the record — if the next run shows SH stamping true on
premises the answer then dies on, that reads directly out of the log.

### Endgame

Slots spent with a load-bearing premise REFUTED: **the run returns nothing.** The REFUTED
block never lifts (the UNVERIFIED block still does). No answer and a wrong answer both
score 0, so letting SH answer on dead ground buys nothing and writes a known-false premise
into the case file for every later question on the dataset.

### `definition` is deleted

`KINDS` becomes `("coverage", "selection", "other")`.

The distinction was wrong. `p3` — *"whether it is the wall-clock span `max(fes)-min(fss)`
or the sum of per-flow durations is unresolved and changes the answer"* — was called
unfalsifiable by search. It is not: *do these records overlap in time* is a query. The five
`ws*.coinhive.com` flows overlap, so the sum (1758) double-counts and the span is the
measure. The genuinely interpretive part — *which records are the act* — is a **selection**
claim and always was. `p12` was a selection claim wearing the definition label, which is
exactly why it could be "verified": selection claims are quotable, definitions are not.

A measurement ambiguity is now an **open question from the senior to SH** — existing
machinery, hard-gated, semantically exact. r2's `p3` should have been `q3`.

The paragraph at `sh_loop.py:170` is relocated, not deleted. Its closing sentence —
*"Records that overlap in time cannot be added without counting the same moments twice"* —
is generic interval arithmetic, the same category as the scope-first and compute-in-SPL
rules, and it is the instruction that separates 1758 from 1666.

### Selection stays singular, and must name its rival

One open premise per kind per author is kept — it is what killed r1's re-file loop. Two
changes:

1. **A selection premise must name at least one rival record set it beats.** The prompt
   already asks for this in prose ("why this entity and not another that could fit"); it
   becomes a schema requirement. `s1` in r1 held two live record sets — the dp=3333 flow
   and the six CoinHive flows — and the ledger had no way to hold both, so one became a
   premise and the other stayed prose in a report and was argued away.
2. **No more silent absorption.** A draft folded into an existing open premise of the same
   kind is reported back in that turn's feedback ("you already have an open selection
   premise p2; amend or settle it") instead of having its text discarded.

## Not changed, ruled on explicitly

- **The coverage premise stays required only at ANSWER.** Requiring one at the first SPAWN
  was considered and declined: at spawn time SH has no data, so its coverage list would come
  from priors — and *"dp=3333 is the canonical Monero pool port"* is precisely a prior. The
  post-hoc property of the ledger is accepted for now.
- **The two-candidate scheme is deferred.** On a REFUTED premise the answer dies rather than
  surviving as one of two candidates for SH to choose between. r1 is the reason: SH already
  had 112 and 1667 side by side in `s1`'s round-5 report and took 112. A formal mechanism for
  that choice formalises a judgement we have watched fail.

## Risks accepted, in the open

1. **SH can stamp everything true and the mechanism never fires.** Deliberate — "trust the
   stamp". The bet is that a specific per-premise question (*does this quote establish this
   claim?*) asked one round before the answering turn is materially harder to wave through
   than one word. If it is not, the stamps are on the record and the failure is diagnosable
   rather than mysterious.
2. **Honest stamping costs a senior slot.** See Budget.
3. **The stamp is still SH.** It is a better question asked at a better moment of the same
   interested party. It is not independence; independence remains the validator's job.

  stamp. `nominatable` is deliberately narrow — what SH stamped false, or what that

  it. The stamp shows in `render_table` and in `premise_ledger.json`, which also now
  carries `rival`: the second live record set is only useful post-run if it is in the
  dump.

  The guard sits in `apply`, not only in the schema, because `PremiseDraft.quote` is a
  second route to VERIFIED and it routes through the same call. Consequence taken
  deliberately: a premise SH files now starts UNVERIFIED and stays there until a senior
  or a validator settles it, so SH must file it early enough for a senior to reach —
  `render_for_senior` already carries load-bearing premises filed by others to every
  active senior.
- **The stamp is mandatory** (`conversation.PremiseStamp`,
  `SeniorDirective.premise_stamps`, `stamp_violations`). A turn that leaves a
  newly-claimed verification unread is rejected. It is due the round the verification is
  claimed, not at ANSWER: for r2 that puts `p1`'s stamp at round 3, where SH graded
  `R3=WEAK` and was still investigating — one round before the moment R4 has flipped to
  PASS in every run we have. The turn is judged as a whole, so SH may stamp in one entry
  and route in another. Stamps are applied only on an accepted turn, like the
  open-question answers: a rejected turn's stamps would burn the one chance each premise
  gets.
- **R4 is a runner-enforced ceiling** (`conversation.r4_ceiling`,
  `grade_ceiling_violations`). SH cannot write PASS while a load-bearing premise
  authored by that senior is UNVERIFIED, or while a stamp on its verifications reads
  false; a REFUTED load-bearing premise forces FAIL. SH may always grade lower, never
  above. R4 now records and triggers nothing, and the clause that taught SH the grade
  was inert — *"the ANSWER gate is the ledger, not this grade"* — is gone from its
  description and from the prompt. The measurement behind it: across three runs and six
  seniors, R4 flipped to PASS on the turn SH stopped investigating, without exception.
  This turn's stamps count toward the ceiling, not only the ledger's, so SH cannot stamp
  a verification false and grade that senior's ground sound in the same breath.
