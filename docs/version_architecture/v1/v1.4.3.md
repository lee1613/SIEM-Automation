# v1.4.3 — the validation agent

**In progress.** Spec: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-20-validation-agent-design.md`
(spec 2 of 2). Opened 2026-09-21, straight after the Q216 ledger runs recorded in
`v1.4.2.md`, because those runs showed the premise ledger cannot catch its own worst
failure.

v1.4.2 phase 2 made premises structural: the runner owns them, they cannot be dropped by
omission, and a VERIFIED status needs a quote from a result the author really received.
Two Q216 runs then showed what that still does not buy. The author of a premise is also
its only witness, and a quote can be genuine, non-circular, and still not establish the
claim it is attached to. v1.4.3 adds the one party the design has never had: a reader who
wants nothing.

## Changelog

- **The validation agent (`agent/v1/validator.py`, new).** An agent handed one settled
  premise and the evidence offered for it, and nothing else — not the question, not the
  reports, not the candidate, not the other premises. It re-checks the claim against the
  data itself and files one `premise_updates` verdict, under the same quote and
  circularity rules as any other author, with its quote checked against its OWN tool
  output.

  It exists because both Q216 ledger runs failed the same way from opposite sides: in r2
  s1 filed all three premises and verified all three itself; in r1 SH filed 18 and
  verified 15 of its own. The ANSWER gate is satisfied by a premise whose author is also
  its only witness, and no prompt fixes that — neither party is disinterested.

  What it has to catch is r2's `p1`: a coverage premise whose own text says route (c) was
  *"NOT yet searched"*, marked VERIFIED quoting the result of searching route (c) and
  returning 4,832 flows where the answer assumed one. `quote_supported` passes (the quote
  is real) and `CIRCULAR` passes (it does not cite SH). Reading a premise against its
  quote is comprehension, not a string test.

- **Settle-time trigger, replacing the spec's staleness trigger**
  (`sh_loop._run_validators`). Spec 2 §3.1 originally fired on a load-bearing premise that
  had been UNVERIFIED for two rounds with the candidate unchanged. r2 settled every
  premise within two rounds of filing it, so that trigger would never have fired. The
  runner now validates a load-bearing premise the moment it leaves UNVERIFIED, before the
  status is allowed to stick. SH is not asked and cannot decline.

  The senior is **not** retired, unlike the spec's version — that suited a trigger firing
  late in a senior's life; this one fires the round after a premise is filed, so retiring
  would end every senior in round 2. Validators run as their own parallel wave inside the
  same SH turn, and their verdicts reach SH with that wave's reports.

- **Budget** (`MAX_VALIDATORS_PER_QUESTION = 6`, `VALIDATOR_ITERS = 8`). Six because r2
  produced three load-bearing premises; it exists to bound a pathological question, not to
  ration a normal one. A premise is validated once, not once per wave.

- **Failure paths all leave the premise alone.** A validator that files no verdict, files
  several, quotes something it did not run, cites SH, or crashes in transport leaves the
  status untouched and says so in SH's next turn. `_timed` turns a crash into an
  `api_failed` dict with none of `validate()`'s keys, so the premise is read from the job
  map rather than the result.

- **`SPECIALISTS["validator"]`** carries the prompt; `parse_specialist_tag` deliberately
  does not match `[validator]`, because only the runner spawns one.

Tests: `test_validator.py` (12) builds on r2's `p1` verbatim — briefing contents, the id
and kind being withheld, the placeholder-id substitution, and every refusal path.
`test_sh_loop.py` gains 4 integration tests: a validator fires without SH being asked and
overturns the author's own VERIFIED, the briefing carries neither the question nor the
candidate, a refuted premise blocks the answer resting on it, and a premise is validated
once. Two `test_sh_loop` doubles were taught the validator role so validator calls stop
counting as senior rounds. 598 passed, 5 skipped, ruff clean.

**Not yet measured.** No run has been made on v1.4.3. The next Q216 run is its acceptance
test, and the signal to read is whether a validator refutes `p1` — the premise that made
112 look settled.
